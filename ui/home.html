Availazy · For volunteer coordinators · Open source

Local AI for Coordinators Who Have Better Things to Do

Let AI read. You decide.

Volunteers answer in their own words: offers, corrections, cancellations, maybes. Availazy reads a week of messages on your own computer, puts each suggestion beside the message it came from and keeps every change on the books. Only what you accept reaches the final sheet.

01

01The problem

Nobody replies in rows and columns.

A coordinator asks “when can you help?” The answers arrive as ordinary messages: “after 3”, “next Tuesday”, “maybe Saturday if my sister can babysit”. Then people change their minds, cancel and repeat themselves, and someone reconciles it all into a spreadsheet by hand.

The error that matters most is the silent one: a cancellation or a condition that never reaches the sheet, so you count on someone who isn't coming.

02

02How it works

The model reads. The code keeps the books. You decide.

The model's job is kept deliberately small: it copies words. Dates, times, history and counts are ordinary Python, and they come out the same every time.

  1. AGemma 4 E2B copies

    Running in Ollama on your laptop, the model copies phrases out of each message (who, which day, what time, any condition) into JSON that has to match a schema. It never works out a date.

    Priya Nair m03
    “Monday after 3 works, and Wednesday afternoon.”

    { "person": "SENDER",
  "day_text": "Monday",
  "time_text": "after 3",
  "status": "available",
  "condition": "",
  "replaces_earlier": false,
  "excerpt": "Monday after 3 works" }

    The first of two objects in the saved Gemma 4 output for m03 (prompt v3); the second copies “Wednesday” and “afternoon”. 3.7 s, 110 output tokens.

  2. BPython decides

    Code turns the phrases into dates and times, checks each suggestion's words against the message, keeps every person's history day by day and keeps conditional offers conditional. It does every count.

    How the phrases resolve
    MondayMon 9 Nov
    after 315:00–20:00
    WednesdayWed 11 Nov
    afternoon13:00–17:00
    • Both quotes found word for word in the message
    • Wednesday flagged: “Model and rules read this differently”

    The planning day runs 09:00–20:00, so “after 3” ends at 20:00

  3. CYou approve

    Each suggestion sits beside its message with the quote highlighted. Accept, reject or edit it, then mark the message reviewed. Only accepted records reach the final sheet.

    AcceptRejectEditMark message reviewed
    Rows in the final sheet
    Priya · Mon 915:00–20:00
    Priya · Wed 1113:00–17:00

    After accepting both and marking m03 reviewed, the final-sheet export is complete: two confirmed rows, traced to m03.1 and m03.2.

Three readers

  • Model + rules cross-check default: both read every message and disagreements are flagged
  • Model only Gemma's suggestions on their own
  • Rules only a keyword baseline that needs no model at all

Why both? On the fictional set A week, Gemma alone left 7 of 12 cancellations on the final sheet. With the rules cross-check: 0 of 12.¹

03Review

Nothing is final until you accept it.

Warnings are written in plain English, and anything unclear waits under Needs a decision instead of being guessed. Review with the mouse or the keyboard. Marking a message reviewed is always your own action, never inferred.

05

05Privacy

Nothing leaves your laptop.

In the core workflow your messages, roster and review decisions live in the browser tab and pass through a small Python server on 127.0.0.1. The model runs in Ollama on the same machine. There is no account, no cloud database and no telemetry by default.

§

Before you rely on it

  1. Frozen first scoring. Cancellation figures count withdrawals still showing on the sheet built from every suggestion in the week, before any human review; for set A that is all 50 messages, development messages included.
  2. Partly luck: “Scratch Wednesday” was missed silently, and the sheet came out right only because an earlier flagged problem had already kept that Wednesday off it.
  3. It records availability (when people say they can or can't help), not shift assignments.
  4. Review work lives only in the browser tab, and reloading replaces it. Saving and reopening a session isn't built yet.
  5. The timezone field is stored but not used, and point times such as “at 9” are not read.
  6. A real quote with invented day or time words is flagged, not held back: it counts on the provisional sheet until you accept or reject it.
  7. All accuracy figures come from fictional messages. No real-coordinator pilot has run, and human time saved has not been measured.
  8. Every name, message and organisation on this page is fictional. Riverside Community Pantry does not exist.
